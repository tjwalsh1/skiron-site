Compute that runs on Iowa wind.

Skiron builds small GPU data centers beside Midwestern wind farms. They run on power the grid can’t carry away, and we rent that computing time to people building and running AI.

Wind the grid can’t use

On windy nights across the Midwest, turbines often make more electricity than the transmission lines can move. When that happens, the grid operator tells wind farms to turn down. The energy they give up is called curtailment.

In MISO, the grid that serves Iowa and much of the Midwest, high wind output caused almost half of all transmission congestion in 2022. Wind curtailment averaged 726 megawatts every hour that year and reached 5.9 gigawatts in some hours.1 MISO curtailment is on pace for a new record in 2026.2

At the same time, demand for AI computing keeps growing, and the large data centers built to meet it can wait years for a grid connection.3

How a Skiron site works

We build one compact building at a wind farm, connected behind the meter to power that’s already there. Inside are used NVIDIA H100 GPU servers, cooling, and the electrical gear to run them. When the wind is strong and the lines are full, our servers put that power to work.

We rent the computing time by the GPU-hour. Some goes to AI startups, researchers and developers through compute marketplaces. Some is sold in longer blocks to cloud providers who need steady capacity. Over time we plan to sign direct contracts with universities and businesses here in Iowa.

What sets Skiron apart

Powered by wind first

Every site sits at a wind farm and draws on its power, much of it energy that would otherwise be curtailed. The farm earns money from electricity it would have lost, and the computing we sell runs on a renewable source you can drive out and see.

Small, so it’s fast

A Skiron site is measured in kilowatts. That size fits within capacity a wind farm already has, so we can skip the multi-year wait large facilities face and be running within a season.

Low-cost power

Buying electricity right at the farm, much of it during curtailment, keeps our biggest operating cost low. That lets us offer competitive prices at a small scale.

Built to be a good neighbor

A lot of rural communities have watched proposals for enormous data centers and had fair questions about land, noise, water, and what happens to everyone’s power bill. We’ve heard those questions, and they shaped how Skiron works.

Each site is one farm-style building next to a wind farm that’s already there. It mainly uses power the grid has no room for, so it adds very little new demand to the wider grid. We want every site to be one a county board is comfortable approving and a neighbor barely notices.

Where this goes

  1. The pilot. One 40-GPU site at an Iowa wind farm, built to prove the model in the field.
  2. More farms. Additional sites across MISO wind country, with newer and more powerful GPUs.
  3. Long-term customers. SOC 2 security certification and multi-year contracts with universities, Iowa businesses and larger technology buyers.
